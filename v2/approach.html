THAW Strategy Consulting · Edmonton

Our approach

Listening first. Then momentum.

Reduced foot traffic, shifting customer preferences, and challenging market conditions require more than short-term fixes. Our purpose is to understand what drives business performance and turn those insights into practical opportunities for renewed momentum and long-term success.

Before the thawEvery engagement starts with the conditions as they are

The method

Five steps, in the order they have to happen.

We do not arrive with a predetermined solution. Each step earns the next one, and the sequence is how a recommendation stays honest from the first conversation to the final measure.

01Listen

Understand the problem from the people who live with it.

We begin with a client's goals, target audiences, operating environment, and constraints, and with what a business is trying to protect, what it is trying to grow, and what it can realistically take on.

Jiahua Lu · Stakeholder relations & business strategy

02Research

Find where, and when, customers go missing.

Primary and secondary research into customer behaviour, market trends, and the barriers that keep people from visiting. We study foot traffic across months, seasons, and times of day, so a slow period is described in data rather than in impressions.

Austin Grimson · Business development & innovation, with Ethan Wang on consumer behaviour and Christine Rideout on barriers to visitation

03Evaluate

Weigh every option against relevance, practicality, and measurable value.

Financial implications, organizational priorities, and practical limitations are considered before anything is recommended. We look at opportunities from multiple perspectives rather than settling on a single solution early.

Christine Rideout · Business strategy & research, with Jiahua Lu on financial feasibility

04Recommend

Deliver recommendations a business can act on, not ideas that look promising on paper.

Clear, coordinated initiatives that connect customer needs with business objectives and implementation realities. Before a recommendation reaches a client, the four of us challenge one another's assumptions and refine it together.

All four of us, together

05Measure

Know whether it is working.

We identify the indicators that show whether a strategy is delivering, so momentum can be tracked and sustained rather than assumed. A good season should be explainable and repeatable.

Tracked by the whole team

What we accomplish

Insight, engagement, strategy.

Three kinds of work for independent, local businesses, where a recommendation is only useful if the owner can act on it.

Market insights

Customer research, foot-traffic analysis by season and time of day, and a clear current-state picture of strengths, challenges, and opportunities.

Marketing & engagement

Campaigns built around what people value, customer loyalty initiatives, and partnerships that let neighbouring businesses bring customers to one another.

Business strategy

Implementation planning, resource and feasibility review, and the stakeholder collaboration that turns a plan into something people will carry out.

How we think

Curiosity. Discipline. Respect.

We approach each challenge with curiosity, analytical discipline, and respect for the people affected by our recommendations.

Curiosity

We approach research with an open mind, looking beyond the obvious answer and taking seriously what motivates people, what they value, and why certain experiences bring them back.

Analytical discipline

Financial awareness, data, and a habit of asking what a decision will cost, who it benefits, and how we would know if it worked. Creative ideas are welcome. Unmeasured ones are not finished.

Respect for the people affected

Successful recommendations depend on stakeholder support. We listen to different perspectives, bring them together, and recommend only what the people involved can get behind.

How we decide

Four perspectives, one recommendation.

Christine asks whether an idea is worth the resources it needs. Ethan asks whether the people it is meant for would value it, and whether it would bring them back. Austin asks whether a business could run it on an ordinary weekday, with the staff and systems it already has. Jiahua asks whether the stakeholders who have to carry it will get behind it, and whether the numbers hold.

A recommendation reaches a client only after it has survived all four questions. That is what we mean by different strengths, one shared approach.
